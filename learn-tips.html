Study Techniques
SR

Spaced Repetition

Revisit a topic after 1 day, then 3 days, then a week. Each gap strengthens long-term memory far more than cramming the night before.

AR

Active Recall

Close the textbook and write down everything you remember. The struggle to recall, not the re-reading, is what builds memory.

25

Pomodoro Sessions

Study hard for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break. After 4 rounds, take a 20-minute break. Keeps focus sharp over long sessions.

TL

Teach to Learn

Explain a concept to a friend (or to an imaginary student). If you can't explain it simply, you haven't understood it well enough yet.

MM

Mind Maps

Draw a central topic and branch out all related sub-topics. Especially powerful for Social Science chapters packed with people, events, and dates.

PP

Past Paper Practice

Solve at least 3 years of board papers under timed conditions. Boards repeat question patterns. Recognising them is half the battle.

Subject-Specific Tips
M

Mathematics

Never skip steps. Board examiners award step marks. Write each formula before substituting values. Daily practice beats weekend marathons.

Sc

Science

Draw labelled diagrams even when not asked. They help recall, and examiners reward clarity. For equations, balance both sides carefully.

En

English

For writing, spend 5 minutes planning before writing. For literature, memorise key quotes, as they earn full marks in passage-based questions.

SS

Social Science

Create chapter-wise timelines for History. For Geography, mark maps daily. A few minutes each day beats a panic session the night before.

Revision Strategy
PL

Plan Your Revision

Divide each subject into chunks and assign them to specific days. A clear timetable removes decision fatigue and ensures nothing is skipped.

WS

Focus on Weak Spots

Identify which topics you keep getting wrong by reviewing your MCQ sessions. Spend 60% of revision time on weak chapters, not your strong ones.

1P

One-Page Notes

After finishing a chapter, condense everything onto a single A4 sheet. These sheets become your rapid-review cheat-sheet in the final week.

Zz

Sleep Is Non-Negotiable

Memory consolidation happens during sleep. Cutting it short to study more is counterproductive. Aim for 7–8 hours during exam preparation.

Exam Day Checklist
1

Read all questions first. Spend the first 15 minutes reading the entire paper. Mark the questions you are most confident about.

2

Attempt confident questions first. This secures marks early and warms up your brain for harder ones.

3

Watch the clock. Budget time per section. Don't let a single question eat your entire paper time.

4

Write neatly and in points. Clear handwriting and bulleted answers are easier for examiners to award full marks.

5

Leave 10 minutes to review. Check for skipped questions, missing labels on diagrams, and unit errors in Maths/Science.

Practice Right Now

Apply these tips immediately. Jump to a subject and start practising.
